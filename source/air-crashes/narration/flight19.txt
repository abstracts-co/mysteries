Fort Lauderdale, Florida, December fifth, 1945. Five Navy Avengers leave for a routine training flight, with fourteen men aboard. Their route is a triangle out over the Bahamas.

After the turn, another instructor overhears them. They are talking about their compasses, and about being lost.

The flight leader reports both his compasses out. He believes he is over the Florida Keys. If so, north would lead home. If he is really over the Bahamas, north leads out to sea.

He passes a small island, with no other land in sight.

They try northeast, then east, then west. He says they will fly west until they reach land, or run out of fuel.

The sun sets at Fort Lauderdale. Weather over the sea is getting worse.

Radio stations finally place them within about 100 miles of a point off northern Florida. The estimate reaches the base, but it is never broadcast to the flight.

Earlier, a warning was relayed that they might soon have to land on the water.

Just after seven in the evening, one pilot calls the leader. There is no answer. It is the last call anyone logged.

A Mariner search plane took off with thirteen men. Twenty-three minutes later, a passing tanker saw flames on the sea. The Navy first blamed the flight leader, then withdrew that, and recorded the cause as unknown. No trace of any of the six aircraft has been confirmed. The Bermuda Triangle story came later. The records describe a lost formation, rough seas, and radio trouble.
