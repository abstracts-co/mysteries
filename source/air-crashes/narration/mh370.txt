Kuala Lumpur, about forty minutes after midnight on March eighth, 2014. Malaysia Airlines 370 departs for Beijing with 239 people aboard.

The crew reports cruising at 35,000 feet. No problem is announced.

Controllers hand the flight over to Ho Chi Minh. The captain's reply is the last recorded voice transmission from this aircraft. "Good night, Malaysian Three Seven Zero."

Just over a minute later, near a waypoint called Igari, the last position broadcast goes out. Its symbol soon disappears from the controllers' screens.

But military radar is still watching. The aircraft has turned back across Malaysia, with no further recorded voice transmission.

Near Penang, the first officer's phone registers with the network. There is no call and no message.

Northwest of Sumatra, military radar sees it one last time. This is the last position that exists.

Three minutes later, the satellite terminal logs back on, probably after a power interruption. What interrupted the satellite link is unknown.

The airline calls the aircraft by satellite. The equipment responds. No one picks up.

From here, there is no picture of this flight. There is only a brief automatic signal, about once an hour, which places the aircraft somewhere on a curve. The path you are watching is one published model of where it went, among many.

On the ground, Kuala Lumpur activates its rescue center, hours after the last contact.

A second satellite call goes unanswered.

Dawn is reaching the southern Indian Ocean. The aircraft answers one more routine satellite check.

Eight minutes later, it asks to reconnect. Those last transmissions suggest a rapid descent. They do not say when or where it ended. After this, there is nothing.

In 2015, a wing part washed up on Réunion Island. More debris followed, on shores across the western Indian Ocean. The first seabed search covered over 120,000 square kilometers. Another surveyed over 112,000, partly overlapping it, and further surveys continued in 2026. They have not found the aircraft. Investigators say the turn back was likely made by hand. Who did it, and why, is not known.
