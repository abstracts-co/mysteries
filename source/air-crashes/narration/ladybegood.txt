Libya, April fourth, 1943. A new American bomber called Lady Be Good takes off on its first mission, to bomb Naples. Nine men are aboard. Blowing sand breaks up the formation.

Alone, the crew turns for home after dark, and releases its bombs. Where they fell isn't known.

The crew reports its direction finder isn't working, and asks for a radio bearing. Later investigators suggested they mistook which side of the station they were on.

Around midnight, men near the base hear a bomber pass overhead, and fire flares. It was probably this one. Investigators believed they flew on into the Sahara, mistaking the desert for the sea.

Two hours later, the fuel runs out.

They jump into the dark and land in desert, not water. Eight of the nine find each other.

The empty bomber flies on, and comes down on a gravel plain, largely intact.

They may have thought the coast was close. It was hundreds of kilometers away. They walked north with half a canteen of water, sharing it by the capful, and left arrows made of parachutes behind them. Diaries record the days. Five stopped at the dunes. Three went on, for help. Oil surveyors found the bomber in 1958. Later searches found the crew's trail, and remains and diaries followed in 1960.
